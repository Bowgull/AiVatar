Instead of the bars, a tiny looping picture plays in the screen. Four of them now. The VIS key goes BARS → SCENE → OFF; in SCENE, click the screen for the next one. Six hand-drawn frames each, about 9 frames a second, and they stop when the player is hidden or a game is full screen.

Why the pier for the fourth: the other three are a street, a room and a forest; this is open water, and it is the only still one (the skate moves, the pier sits). Blue hour with one warm lantern is the same cold-world-warm-light feel as the rain and the fire, and water is where pixel colour-cycling looks best. It is also the fishing spot from every cosy game. (Also tried in my head: a night-train window, too close to the rain window; a lantern market, too busy to read at this size; snow at a cabin, too close to the campfire.)

?WHAT EACH PART DOES

PinThe same pin as the job pop-out. Pin one pop-out and they all stay on top. Hover by it for the see-through slider; every pop-out follows. FoldDouble-click anywhere (or the minus key) for the thin strip: scrolling title, play/pause, a progress line. Double-click again to come back. VIS keyBARS, SCENE or OFF. Off by default if you prefer it calm; it remembers. In SCENE, click the screen for the next scene. Seek and volumeGrab the gold bead and drag; the big time follows in the screen. The volume knob is Spotify's own volume. Where the song comes fromSpotify's official sign-in, plus Windows' "now playing" so it shows the song even before you sign in.
How these were made
  • The player is new, so it is drawn: every wood, paper, key and icon pixel placed by hand at exactly 2×, using Samara's own wood pictures and the bubble's paper colours.
  • The seek and volume bars are the app's own round progress bar (gold, cream). The pin is the job pop-out's pin. Round corners are drawn pixel by pixel, never smoothed.
  • The four scenes (sheet-59/scenes.js) are made-up pixel paintings built the way the pixel-loop artists build theirs (Waneella, Kirokaze, Mark Ferrari's colour cycling), not copied from them: a small palette per scene, ONE light source and everything else in shadow, people and the cat lit on the side facing the light so they read, light as two or three flat bands (no noisy dither), cycling colours for water, rain and fire, and small 2-4 frame motions (steam, a tail, a bobber). They would ship as small sprite sheets; the skate's street layers would scroll as endless strips.
  • In section 4 the video pop-out (with 12 px corners, the one change asked for) and the Claude job are drawn by the app's own styles. The cover art is a made-up pixel painting.
