EIGHT DIVIDERS — THE DIAMOND WAS THE LAZY ONE

Each does a different job. A divider is not decoration; it says what kind of break this is.

1 — CARVED GROOVE · a channel cut into the surface, not a line drawn on it. The default.

2 — FORGED END-CAPS · the rule terminates in metal. For a major section break.

3 — TORN PAGE · the parchment itself ends. For "that is everything".

4 — DOUBLE RULE · Final Fantasy's thin pair, unequal weights. Quiet, for inside a section.

5 — CHAIN · links. For two things that are connected — before and after, cause and effect.

6 — GEM IN A SETTING · a faceted stone with real highlights, not a flat diamond. For something important.

7 — HEADING ON THE RULE · the rule runs behind and the words sit on it. Replaces a heading AND a divider.

8 — STITCHED · dashed, informal. For something provisional or a note to self.

THE ONE CAVEAT

From the same source: "embossed text routinely lands under the WCAG 4.5:1 minimum." Letterpress works by lowering contrast, so the carved headings here use a darker ink than looks strictly prettiest — it has to stay readable at a glance on a bright screen with a game behind it. Worth checking rather than assuming, because this is exactly the trade where style quietly wins over legibility.
