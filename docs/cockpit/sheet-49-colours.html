SHEET 49 · WHAT EACH COLOUR MEANS

Eight marks can sit over Aang's head. Each one has to be a different colour, and the colour should say what kind of thing it is before you read the shape. Below: today's colours (with the problems), then two fixed palettes built on colour theory. His aura breathes in the mark's colour only for the marks that want you.

THE RULES (COLOUR THEORY, PLAINLY)

  • Cool colours ask, warm colours tell. Blue and violet read as "stop and look"; amber and gold read as "news, no rush". So anything that needs you is cool.
  • Green means done, red means wrong. Every game and app has trained this; fighting it costs a second of thinking each time.
  • Same family, different strength. A step done and the whole job done are both "done": same green family, the step light and small, the job deep and big.
  • Grey means nothing to do. Paused is the only mark with no colour of its own.
  • Who vs what. Colours that say WHO (Claude's terracotta, Aang's gold, the plum keys) stay on cards and buttons. Colours that say WHAT IS HAPPENING live only on the marks, the aura and the pinned ring. Never one colour for both.
  • Shape backs up colour. Every mark already has its own shape (key, ?, scroll, ticks, hourglass, skull), so someone colour-blind still tells them apart.
