FIRST, SOMETHING EVERY SHEET SO FAR GOT WRONG

Sheets 1 to 8 drew Aang standing on the floor beside a bubble. That is not where it goes. Reading anchor.ts, which was itself read out of the C# that draws it today: the bubble's bottom-right corner sits 16px right of his left edge and 38px below his top, and the panel grows up and left from there, so that one corner never moves. It hangs at his shoulder, just overlapping him. The typing box starts 240px left of him, which puts its left and right edges on exactly the same two lines as the bubble. That is what makes them a pair, and it was true before anyone drew it.

Everything below is at full size, with his real sprite, placed by those numbers.

1 · THE THOUGHT TAIL BUILT

How that era drew one, which is the same in comics and in every 16 and 64 bit game that borrowed from them:

  • Separate round beads, not one shape. A speech tail is attached. A thought tail is a trail of independent circles with a visible gap between each, and that gap is the whole difference between thinking and talking.
  • Three, shrinking. Odd number, almost always three, each smaller than the last. Two reads as a mistake; four crowds him.
  • Each wears the full edge. Same near-black outline, same fill, same hard contact shadow as the panel. In pixel art the outline thins on the smallest bead so it does not turn into a solid blob, which is done here: 4px, 4px, 3px.
  • They arc. A straight row reads as a dotted line. A curve reads as a thought leaving him.
  • They hang off the corner that never moves. Because the panel grows up and left, anchoring the beads to the bottom-right corner means they stay on his head whether the reply is one line or twenty, with no maths at all.

2 · THE TYPING BOX, MIXED PICK ONE

You asked whether it can be a mix of all three. It can, and it is the better answer. A Stardew input is not one flat colour: it is a wooden frame with a field set into it. So the wood becomes the furniture, the field becomes the thing you write on, and the only question left is what the field is made of.
